# 06 · Outreach and proposal playbook

## Route each business to an appropriate offer

Choose a relevant service based on a verified observation, the business category, and what the agency can deliver. Use an estimated budget to guide internal preparation; never tell a prospect “our software says you can afford $25,000.” Confirm business priorities and budget in conversation.

Initial cold outreach is a short proposal to investigate one improvement. A detailed priced proposal follows discovery. Sending a generic attachment with every service to every business is unlikely to establish relevance.

## Research checklist per account

- Verify business identity, current location/service area, and appropriate contact role.
- Find one recent, specific, supportable observation; record URL and observation date.
- Identify an appropriate offer and a cautious business benefit to discuss.
- Check source usage rights, channel eligibility, verification, suppression, and account contact history.
- Select approved portfolio proof only when true, relevant, and authorized for use.
- Keep full budget reasoning internal. Do not disclose guessed revenue or personal information.

Do not claim to have run an audit if only a homepage was checked. Do not invent a conversation, referral, relationship, traffic estimate, ad spend, ranking loss, or business problem.

## Default sequence

This cadence is an initial internal limit, subject to provider permission and observed recipient response. It is not a safe-volume guarantee.

| Day | Action | Conditions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | One reviewed email, or a human business call | Choose an eligible channel; one account owner |
| 5 | Brief email follow-up with relevant additional context | No reply/opt-out; all pre-send checks rerun |
| 12 | Final brief follow-up | Same conditions; stop after this step |
| Optional | Up to two human call tasks instead of some email touches | Screened business number and applicable state/channel rules |

Default: at most 3 emails per contact and 4 combined sales touches per account in 21 days. Call tasks replace touches where needed; they do not add unlimited contact attempts. One active account-level sequence at a time. Stop on any reply, complaint, bounce, opt-out, channel restriction, or lost eligibility. No automatic re-enrollment after the sequence; revisit only with a documented new reason and eligibility review after a default 90-day quiet period. Suppressions always survive that period.

## Message components

Use an accurate subject, a real sender, one relevant observation, one offer, and one low-pressure question. Target roughly 60–120 words in the body before the required footer. Plain text is sufficient. Start without attachments, tracking pixels, shortened links, or a calendar link. Offer to send a short outline; introduce scheduling when there is interest.

Every message and follow-up must contain the actual commercial disclosure, sender identity, valid postal address, and functional opt-out. The renderer appends the reviewed footer automatically; missing configuration or unresolved placeholders blocks sending. See the [CAN-SPAM requirements](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business).

### Template A: website opportunity

```text
Subject: Website idea for {{business_name}}

Hi {{first_name_or_team}},

I'm {{sender_name}} from {{agency_name}}. While reviewing your public
website, I noticed {{verified_observation}}.

We help {{business_category}} businesses with websites and online
customer inquiries. We could put together a short outline for
{{specific_improvement}} so you can decide whether it is useful.

Would you like me to send it over?

{{sender_name}}
{{required_footer}}
```

Use “while reviewing your public website” only when the record includes that review. For a missing-site lead, replace the observation with “I couldn't find a business-owned website in the sources I checked; do you have one?” Absence from a search is not proof that no site exists.

### Template B: trades, SEO, or paid search

```text
Subject: An idea for {{business_name}} in {{service_area}}

Hi {{first_name_or_team}},

I saw {{verified_service_or_expansion_fact}} on your website.
Our agency helps {{trade_category}} businesses improve service pages,
local search visibility, and inquiry handling.

Would a short review of {{specific_page_or_journey}} be useful?
We can focus on that one area before discussing a broader engagement.

{{sender_name}}
{{required_footer}}
```

### Template C: medical, legal, or real estate practice

```text
Subject: {{practice_or_team_name}} website inquiry

Hi {{first_name_or_team}},

I'm {{sender_name}} with {{agency_name}}. I noticed
{{verified_public_business_observation}}.

We work on business websites and online inquiry journeys. I have
an idea for {{specific_non_sensitive_improvement}} and can send a
brief outline if your team is considering improvements in that area.

Is there someone who handles your website or marketing?

{{sender_name}}
{{required_footer}}
```

No patient, legal case, or property-client data belongs in these messages. Follow the sector's advertising requirements when drafting claims or future client campaigns.

### Follow-up and final touch

```text
Subject: {{accurate_original_subject}}

Hi {{first_name_or_team}},

Following up on my note about {{specific_improvement}}.
{{one_additional_verified_detail_if_useful}}

Would you like the short outline, or should I close this out?

{{sender_name}}
{{required_footer}}
```

```text
Subject: {{accurate_original_subject}}

Hi {{first_name_or_team}},

I'll close out my notes on {{specific_improvement}} for now.
If it becomes a priority, you're welcome to reply here.

{{sender_name}}
{{required_footer}}
```

Use actual email threading only for actual earlier messages. Never add a fake `Re:` or `Fwd:` to imply a conversation.

### Required footer pattern

```text
{{agency_legal_or_trade_name}} | {{valid_physical_postal_address}}
Business advertisement for our agency services.
Prefer no further marketing emails? Reply “unsubscribe” or use
{{working_unsubscribe_url}}.
```

The deployed footer must use real agency details and a tested endpoint. Keep opt-out handling active after the last message. Channel-specific legal detail is centralized in [document 07](07-deliverability-and-channel-rules.md).

## Human calling script

“Hi, this is [name] from [agency]. We help local businesses with websites and marketing. Is now a bad time for a brief business inquiry? I noticed [verified observation], and wanted to ask who handles [relevant service] for your business.”

If relevant: ask what they want to improve, who decides, timing, and whether they want a short outline by email. If declined: thank them and record the scope of the do-not-contact request. Use real caller ID. Do not use AI voices, prerecorded calls, or automatic dialing in the MVP. Call recording is off unless its applicable consent requirements have been addressed.

## Discovery questions

1. Which services or locations do you want to grow, and what does a valuable customer look like?
2. How are inquiries generated and followed up today?
3. What is working, and where is the main bottleneck?
4. Who approves marketing work, and when could a project start?
5. What monthly service budget or project range is practical? Is advertising spend separate?
6. What access, tracking, reporting, brand, or sector restrictions must we account for?

## Detailed proposal structure

Prepare a reviewed proposal with business goal, verified current-state observations, selected services, deliverables, exclusions, timeline, responsibilities, service fees, one-time fees, separately funded media, measurement plan, dependencies, commercial terms, and next steps.

Offer a focused option and, when relevant, an expanded option. Quote $25k+ only when scope and delivery capacity support it. Use baseline and target metrics with explicit assumptions; do not guarantee rankings, leads, revenue, or profit. Record the accepted version and hand it to a delivery owner.
